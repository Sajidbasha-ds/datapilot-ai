College Mid-Exam Proven Strategy Guide

Master time allocation, tactical skipping, intelligent guessing, and mistake reduction.

⏱️ Section 1: Quantitative Aptitude (30 Qs / 30 Min)

Pacing: Exactly 1 minute per question. Do not spend more than 90 seconds on any single numerical calculation.

  • Scan first: Solve pure formula questions (unit conversion, direct speed-distance-time) in the first 10 minutes.
  • Use relative speed addition ($S_1 + S_2$) for opposite directions and subtraction ($S_1 - S_2$) for same direction.
  • In average speed problems, never take simple average unless times are equal! Use $2xy/(x+y)$.

📝 Section 2: Verbal Ability (30 Qs / 30 Min)

Pacing: 40–50 seconds per question. Save 5 minutes at the end for reviewing flagged items.

  • Articles: Always pronounce the initial sound rather than looking at the spelling letter (e.g. an honest man, a university).
  • Tenses: Look for past marker clues ("before", "by the time", "since"). Remember: earlier past action takes Past Perfect (had + V3).
  • Passive Voice: Check verb tense consistency and ensure the subject-verb agreement matches the new passive subject.

💻 Section 3: Technical (10 Pseudocode + 3 Coding / 60 Min)

Pacing: 20 minutes for 10 Pseudocode questions (2 min/Q) + 40 minutes for 3 Coding problems.

  • Pseudocode: Draw a 3-column trace table (Line, Condition, Variables Watch) on scratch paper to avoid off-by-one errors.
  • Break & Continue: Carefully trace which loop level (inner vs outer) is broken or continued.
  • Coding: Always test edge cases (e.g., $N = 0$, negative numbers, division by zero) before final submission.
